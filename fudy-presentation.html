Fudy Order & Pay

Snapshot

  • Lead Product Designer on Fudy’s product transformation.
  • Transition from food delivery to hospitality sales platform.
  • Delivered QR-based Order & Pay for venues in ~10 months.

Focus of this case

  • Design system rebuild to support speed and consistency.
  • Component library, tokens and theming for white-label needs.
  • Cross-platform context: iOS, Android and Web.







Project context

  • Designed user-facing and kitchen-facing web experiences.
  • Built foundations for reusable components in Figma.
  • Created supporting assets, including QR sticker templates.

Order & Pay product snapshots

1) What was the business goal and the design challenge?

  • Business goal: ship a scalable white-label product fast while keeping UX quality high.
  • System goal: create one source of truth for design decisions.
  • Main challenge: existing design system was rushed and inconsistent.
  • Complexity: separate codebases (iOS, Android, Web) increased drift.
  • Risk: without a better system, speed and consistency would keep dropping.

Before: component inconsistency and complexity

2) Which approaches did you explore, and how did you make a decision?

  • Compared patching the old library vs rebuilding core foundations.
  • Explored style-based setup vs token/variable-based setup.
  • Used Figma Variables as the decision trigger for a rebuild.
  • Migrated styles to primitives first, then semantic aliases by usage.
  • Applied atomic + modular component structure for reuse and maintainability.
  • Decision criteria: scalability, clarity for dev handoff, and theming flexibility.

Migration to token-driven foundations

Atomic and modular component architecture

3) How did you know it was the right direction?

  • System became clearer and easier to scale than the old setup.
  • Token structure enabled faster theming and brand adaptation.
  • Component logic became more reusable across product surfaces.
  • Design-development handoff improved with a shared token language.
  • Outcome: stronger base for efficient and transparent product delivery.

Theming and white-label readiness

Reflection

  • Design systems must move deliberately to keep product teams fast.
  • Early investment in architecture prevents later delivery slowdowns.
  • The biggest value came from balancing structure with real shipping needs.
